The question

Make noisy EMG and knee-angle data useful for understanding each repetition.

What I did

Cleaned signals, detected reps, engineered features and compared model baselines.

Why it matters

A reproducible pipeline with readable per-rep summaries and explicit evaluation limits.

Technical notes & decisionsPreprocessing, validation and model results

From files to features

Built flexible loaders for inconsistent delimiters, headers and decimal formats. Cleaned and filtered EMG, then used the knee-goniometer channel to detect repetition boundaries. Computed activation metrics per repetition and extracted modelling features in 200 ms windows with a 50 ms hop.

The decisions that mattered

  • Robust normalisation: used the 95th percentile instead of the single maximum. Without MVC calibration, the output describes relative activation, not a clinical percentage.
  • Adaptive rep detection: smoothed the knee-angle signal with Savitzky–Golay and checked detected peaks against movement, rather than relying on one fixed threshold.
  • Baselines first: compared threshold rules, Logistic Regression, KNN, SVM and Gradient Boosting before selecting a multi-muscle Random Forest.
  • Subject-aware evaluation: used leave-one-subject-out validation to investigate generalisation and avoid letting overlapping windows from one person make a random split look stronger than it was.

Results and limits

Prepared test-set R²: RF 0.967, VM 0.959, BF 0.949, ST 0.915. MAE: 2.75, 2.80, 2.83 and 3.05 respectively, on the processed activation scale. The averages above describe that prepared dataset and split; they are not a guarantee for a new athlete or new hardware.

Next steps: collect MVC calibration, validate on new athletes and exercises, and add uncertainty estimates. This is an analytics foundation, not a medical or diagnostic product.
