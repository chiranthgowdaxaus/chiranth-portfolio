Five surfaces, one identity

Each format asked for something different, so I kept the identity fixed and adjusted scale, contrast and hierarchy for each one.

  • App conceptsProduct, price and add-to-cart readable at phone size, with the brand colour reserved for actions.
  • Social campaignsOne headline that still reads as a small thumbnail in a busy feed.
  • Brochures & in-storeReadable from a few metres away, with one message per panel.
  • PackagingWraps around a three-dimensional box, so the name and logo have to work from any face.
  • Retail labelsScanned in seconds by shoppers who read Kannada, English or both.

The shelf-label system

Every label follows the same four-part structure, so a new product only needs new words and the right category colour.

  1. Category colour. Green for vegetables, orange for fruit, blue for dairy, so an aisle can be read from a distance.
  2. English name. Bold capitals, the first thing most shoppers read.
  3. Kannada name. Optically sized to sit level with the English, not as a smaller afterthought.
  4. Price block. The largest number on the label, in white on the category colour.

Decisions & limitsWhat mattered, and what this work doesn't claim

The decisions that mattered

  • Design for the medium: a social thumbnail, a phone screen and a printed shelf strip need different contrast, scale and spacing.
  • Bilingual hierarchy: balanced Kannada and English with optical sizing and a clear reading order, keeping product names and prices legible.
  • Reusable structure: repeatable layouts so new promotions and labels didn't need a redesign.

Scope and limits

This shows design thinking and visual consistency. It isn't evidence of increased sales, conversion or a deployed app.
