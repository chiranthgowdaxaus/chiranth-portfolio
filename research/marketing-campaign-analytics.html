Overview

The question

Which contacts convert, and what should a campaign team do differently?

What I did

Built conversion views by month, channel, frequency and previous outcome, and kept post-call information out of them.

What it showed

Past success beats any other signal, repeated calls convert less, and the busiest months convert worst.

The data

The public UCI Bank Marketing dataset: 45,211 phone contacts from a Portuguese bank's term-deposit campaigns. Historical data, analysed in Python and SQL.

Definitions

  • Conversion = contacts that subscribed ÷ all contacts in the group.
  • Contacts are observations, not people. The data has no client ID, so one person can appear more than once.
  • Months combine every year in the data.

What I left out, and why

Call duration predicts the outcome almost perfectly, but it's only known after the call ends, so it can't guide who to call. I excluded it, and later campaign outcomes, from anything presented as a planning signal.

Limits

These are descriptive differences. High-converting months had few, likely more selective, contacts, so the month alone doesn't cause the higher rate.
