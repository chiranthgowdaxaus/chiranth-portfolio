I designed and built a bill-tracking app around three questions: what's due, when is it due, and has it been paid?

Overview

The task

Keep recurring bills, payment history and shared costs in one place without making everyday actions hard to find.

What I designed

Four-tab navigation, bill cards, a due-date calendar and distinct states for bills and shared balances.

What I built

The interface, recurring-bill logic, sign-in and stored records. An iOS build is in internal TestFlight testing.

Try the main flow

Home shows bills and their due dates. Each card keeps its payment action in the same place. Marking a bill paid records the payment and creates the next recurring bill once. Paidly records balances; it doesn't move money.

Design decisions

Fewer destinations

I cut six navigation items to four: Home, Calendar, Overview and Settings. Payment history lives in Settings instead of taking a tab.

Room for the action

Bill cards reserve space for the payment button. I adjusted long titles, dates and spacing after checking narrow layouts at 320 and 390 pixels.

A calendar that doesn't jump

Bill markers sit inside each date cell without moving the date number. Selecting a date shows its bills below the calendar.

Separate payment states

Paying a bill to a company is different from friends settling a shared balance. The two stay separate so one action never implies the other.

Design through to code

I designed the flows, built the reusable UI and implemented it. Layout checks caught a header covering content and cramped card actions.

What still needs testing

Syncing splits between two accounts and alerts on physical devices. This is a working design in internal testing, not a measured user outcome.
